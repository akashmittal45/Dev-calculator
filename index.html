How It Works
A guide to every section of the feasibility cockpit
Overview

This application models the financial feasibility of a residential apartment project from a developer's perspective. It answers the key questions: Is this project profitable? How much capital do I need? When do I get it back? What's my return?

1. Project Setup — Land & FSI

Land area (acres) — the total plot.

Base FSI — the permitted floor space index. Multiplied by land area (in sqft) to give the base saleable area. For example: 5 acres × 43,560 sqft/acre × 2.5 FSI = 5,44,500 sqft.

Premium FSI (%) — additional FSI you purchase above base. Entered as a % of base FSI. For example: 40% premium on a 2.5 base = 1.0 additional FSI, giving total FSI of 3.5.

Premium FSI cost (₹/sqft) — the per-sqft cost you pay to the authority for the premium portion. This automatically populates the "Premium FSI cost" line in Cost Build-up.

Total saleable area = Base FSI area + Premium FSI area. This is the number used across pricing, costs, and cashflows.

2. Land Treatment & IRR

Land cost (in Cost Build-up) — actual cash paid for the land. This appears as a real outflow in the cashflow statement.

Land opportunity cost (in Project Setup) — the notional market value of land you already own. No cash moves, so this does NOT appear in cashflow. However, it IS included as a Year 0 outflow when computing IRR. Why? Because deploying land worth ₹X means you're forgoing the option to sell it for ₹X. Without this, IRR on already-owned land looks artificially high.

If buying land afresh: enter the cost in Cost Build-up, leave opportunity cost = 0.
If you already own the land: set Land cost = 0, enter market value as opportunity cost.
Hybrid: both inputs work together — they're additive for IRR purposes.

3. Pricing Bands

Real projects don't sell all units at the same price — early buyers get lower prices, prices escalate as the project gains traction. Pricing bands let you model this:

Each band says "for the first X% of inventory sold, the price is ₹Y/sqft." The blended realisation is the weighted average across all bands. This drives the Total GDV calculation.

The cumulative % in the last band should reach 100% to cover all inventory.

4. Cost Build-up

Land cost — flat ₹ Cr amount. The total purchase price or advance.

Approvals & statutory — per-sqft cost × total saleable area. Covers plan sanction, RERA registration, BBMP/BDA fees, etc.

Premium FSI cost — automatically derived from Project Setup (premium area × cost/sqft). Read-only.

Construction cost — per-sqft cost × total saleable area. The core civil + MEP + finishing cost.

Marketing — flat ₹ Cr, discretionary. Your advertising, sample flat, events budget.

Sales brokerage — % of GDV. Unlike other costs, brokerage is paid on each sale (follows the sales schedule, not construction phasing). It doesn't have user-editable year-wise phasing — it automatically distributes based on when units are sold.

Year-wise phasing (%) — for each cost head (except brokerage), you specify what % of the total is spent in each year. Must sum to 100%.

5. Development Manager Fee & RERA

DM fee — a % of GDV paid to the development manager. It's deducted from customer collections (not a separate cash outflow). The DM receives their share over time as customers pay, following the same RERA escrow rules.

RERA escrow split — under RERA, all customer payments go into an escrow account. Typically 30% is liquid (developer can use freely) and 70% is locked until construction milestones are met. As the project reaches completion %, a corresponding share of the locked amount is released.

Both the developer's portion and the DM's portion follow this 30/70 split.

6. Sales Schedule

All three inputs are cumulative by end of each year, capped at 100%, and never decreasing year-on-year:

Sales % — total inventory sold by that year-end (e.g. 60% means 60% of all units are sold by Year 2).

Collections % — of the total sold value, how much has actually been collected from buyers by that year-end.

Completion % — cumulative construction milestone reached. This is what triggers the release of the 70% locked RERA escrow.

The year-on-year change in (Sales × Collections) gives the actual cash coming in each year as a fraction of GDV.

7. Cashflow & Financing

The cashflow table shows year-by-year money movement, grouped into three sections:

Operating — Inflow (developer's collections released through RERA) minus Outflows (all cost heads phased into that year). Net CF is the operational surplus or shortfall.

Project CF — Net CF minus Interest on outstanding debt. This is the project's true cash position. The Cumulative column tracks the running total — it peaks negative when capital need is highest, and turns positive at break-even.

Financing — Year 0 outflows are equity-funded. From Year 1 onwards, any shortfall (after interest) is covered by construction debt. Surpluses repay debt first, then flow to equity. Interest accrues at the input rate on outstanding debt balance.

Equity CF — what actually flows to/from the equity holder after debt is serviced.

8. IRR

Project IRR — the un-levered internal rate of return. Based on the operational cashflow series (Net CF), ignoring how it's financed. Measures the project's intrinsic return — useful for comparing projects apples-to-apples.

Equity IRR — the levered return on equity invested. Based on the Equity CF series (after debt draws, repayments, and interest). This is your actual return as the equity holder — always higher than Project IRR when debt is used (leverage effect).

Land opportunity cost — when entered, both IRRs are adjusted by adding the notional land value as an additional Year 0 outflow. This gives a more realistic return benchmark for projects on already-owned land.

Hurdle rate — your benchmark return (e.g. 18%). The cockpit flags in green/amber/red whether IRR clears it.

9. Unit Mix (Optional)

By default, total saleable area is derived from FSI. Toggle on the unit mix to override this with a defined apartment configuration (1 BHK, 2 BHK, 3 BHK, etc.).

Each type specifies a count and average size. The total area from the mix is compared against the FSI-derived area — a large gap may indicate the mix doesn't fully utilise available FSI or exceeds it.

Pricing bands still apply on top — the unit mix determines the total area, the pricing bands determine price escalation across the inventory.
