Land treatment

There are two distinct concepts here:

Land cost (in cost build-up) — actual cash you're paying or have committed to pay. This shows up as a real outflow in the cashflow statement. If land is already owned and fully paid, set this to 0.

Land opportunity cost (below) — the notional market value of land you already own. No cash moves, so it does NOT appear in cashflow. But it is treated as a Year 0 outflow when computing IRR — because deploying land worth ₹X to a project means you're forgoing the option to sell it for ₹X. Without this, IRR on already-owned land looks artificially great.

Used in IRR only — set to 0 if buying land afresh
Financing & returns

Year 0 outflows are equity-funded. From Year 1 onwards, any operational shortfall is funded by construction debt at the interest rate below. Surpluses repay debt first, then return to equity. The hurdle rate is your benchmark return for IRR comparison.

Replaces equity at Year 1 start

Debt at launch — if you plan to refinance some of your Year 0 equity with a loan at project launch, enter the amount here. This debt is drawn at Year 1 Q1, reducing your net equity invested. Interest begins accruing immediately. For example: if Year 0 equity is ₹25 Cr and you draw ₹15 Cr at launch, your effective equity is ₹10 Cr.

Quick Start Guide
How to use the Dev Cockpit — step by step
Getting started

Welcome! This cockpit helps you model whether a residential apartment project is financially viable. Here's the fastest path to a working feasibility:

Step 1 → Go to Project Setup. Enter your land area, FSI, and premium FSI. The cockpit will calculate your total saleable area.

Step 2 → Go to Pricing Bands. Set your expected selling prices — you can model price escalation as you sell more inventory.

Step 3 → Go to Cost Build-up. Enter your construction cost per sqft, land cost, marketing budget, and other expenses. Set the DM fee % and RERA escrow split here too.

Step 4 → Check the Sales Schedule. The defaults work for most projects. Adjust if you have a specific velocity plan or pre-launch sales.

Step 5 → Visit Cashflow to see the full year-wise picture. Then check IRR Breakup to see your return analysis.

That's it! The Overview dashboard always shows the latest numbers. Save your work via Saved Projects and export reports via Export.

Step 1 — Project Setup

What to enter:
• Land area — your total plot in acres
• Base FSI — the permitted floor space index (e.g. 2.5)
• Premium FSI % — additional FSI you're purchasing above base (e.g. 40% means 40% extra on top of base). Leave blank if none
• Premium FSI cost — the ₹/sqft you pay the authority for the premium portion. This auto-populates in Cost Build-up
• Total units — how many apartments you plan. Used to calculate average unit size

What to check: Look at the "Derived metrics" card — does the saleable area make sense for your plot?

Land treatment: If you already own the land and didn't pay cash, enter 0 for Land cost in Cost Build-up but put the current market value as "Land opportunity cost" here. This ensures your IRR reflects the true opportunity cost of deploying the land.

Financing: Set the interest rate your construction lender charges (typically 12-14% in India) and your personal hurdle rate (minimum return you'd accept). "Debt at launch" is for when you refinance your Year 0 equity with a loan at project launch.

Timeline: Pick your project start quarter and set the pre-launch gap (how many quarters before construction and sales begin — typically 2-4 quarters for approvals).

Step 2 — Pricing Bands

What to enter:
Each band says "for the first X% of inventory sold, price is ₹Y/sqft." Add multiple bands to model price escalation.

Example:
Band 1: 0-25% sold at ₹10,000/sqft (early buyers, lower price)
Band 2: 25-50% sold at ₹10,500/sqft
Band 3: 50-75% sold at ₹11,000/sqft
Band 4: 75-100% sold at ₹11,500/sqft (late buyers, premium price)

Tips:
• The last band's cumulative % should reach 100%
• Check the "Blended realisation" metrics to see your weighted average price
• Typical Bangalore projects see 5-10% price escalation per phase

Step 3 — Cost Build-up

DM fee & RERA (top of page):
• DM fee = what your development manager charges as % of GDV. They get paid from customer collections, following the same RERA escrow rules
• RERA split = typically 30% liquid (you can use freely) and 70% locked (released as construction completes)

Cost heads:
• Land cost — enter as flat ₹ Cr. If land is already owned, enter 0 here and use opportunity cost in Project Setup
• Approvals & statutory — enter as ₹/sqft. Covers plan sanction, RERA, BBMP/BDA fees
• Premium FSI cost — auto-calculated from your Project Setup inputs. Read-only
• Construction cost — enter as ₹/sqft. This is your core civil + MEP + finishing
• Marketing — flat ₹ Cr for advertising, sample flat, events
• Sales brokerage — % of GDV, paid when units are sold (follows sales schedule)

Year-wise phasing: For each cost head, enter what % of the total is spent in each year. Must add to 100%. Example for construction: Year 0: 0%, Year 1: 20%, Year 2: 40%, Year 3: 30%, Year 4: 10%.

Check: Click "Show calculation" below the cost summary to see the full waterfall from GDV to gross profit.

Step 4 — Sales Schedule

Pre-launch sales (top of page):
If you plan to sell some units before formal launch at a discount, enter the count and discount %. These units are fully collected in Year 0 with no RERA lock.

Year-wise schedule:
All three numbers are cumulative — they represent "by end of this year, what's the total %?"
• Sales % — total inventory sold so far (e.g. Year 2 = 60% means 60% of all units are sold by end of Year 2)
• Collections % — of all sold value, how much has been collected from buyers
• Completion % — construction milestone reached (this triggers release of RERA-locked funds)

How collections drive inflows:
Each year's actual cash inflow = (this year's cumulative Sales × Collections) minus (last year's cumulative Sales × Collections), multiplied by GDV. So if Year 2 is 60% sold × 40% collected = 24% of GDV, and Year 1 was 25% × 30% = 7.5%, then Year 2's collection = 16.5% of GDV.

Tips:
• Numbers should always increase year over year (they're cumulative)
• Last year should ideally reach 100% for both sales and collections
• The cockpit auto-corrects if you accidentally enter a decreasing value

Step 5 — Reading the Cashflow

The cashflow table is grouped into sections:

Operating (Inflow | Outflows | Net CF)
This is the project's operational picture — what comes in from customers (after RERA mechanics) vs what goes out as costs. Net CF is the surplus or shortfall before financing.

Project (Project CF | Cumulative)
Project CF = Net CF minus interest on any outstanding debt. This is the project's true cash position. The Cumulative column shows the running total — it'll start negative (you're spending), dip to its lowest (peak capital requirement), then climb back to positive (break-even).

Financing (Interest | Debt CF | Debt bal.)
Year 0 is fully equity-funded. From Year 1, any shortfall is covered by debt. Surpluses repay debt first. Interest accrues at your input rate on the outstanding balance.

Equity CF
The bottom line — what actually flows to/from you as the equity holder. Negative = you're putting cash in. Positive = cash coming back to you.

Key numbers to focus on:
• Peak Debt — the maximum loan you'll need
• Break-even — when cumulative cashflow turns positive
• Equity IRR — your annualised return on equity invested

Step 6 — IRR Breakup

This tab shows exactly how your return (IRR) is calculated:

Equity capital waterfall — traces your capital from land deployed → Year 0 outflows → pre-launch revenue → debt at launch → net equity invested → returns over time → equity multiple

Quarterly cashflow table — the exact quarterly series used for IRR. Under the hood, your annual inputs are spread into quarterly cashflows for precision. IRR is computed on this series then annualised.

NPV curve — shows NPV at different discount rates. Where the line crosses zero = your IRR. If the curve never crosses, the project doesn't have a positive IRR at that range.

Tip: The cumulative column in the quarterly table includes land cost (cash + opportunity) so you can see the full payback journey from total capital deployed.

Tips & common questions

Q: My IRR looks unrealistically high/low — what's wrong?
Check: (1) Is your equity invested very small relative to returns? High leverage = high IRR. (2) Did you forget to enter land cost or opportunity cost? (3) Are your pricing bands realistic for the micro-market?

Q: The cost phasing warning won't go away
Each cost head's year-wise percentages must sum to exactly 100%. Check if you have a rounding issue (e.g. 33+33+33 = 99, not 100).

Q: Why are my Year 1 inflows so low despite high sales?
Because of RERA — 70% of collections are locked and only released as construction milestones are hit. If your completion % is low in Year 1, most of the locked money stays locked.

Q: Can I model multiple projects?
Yes — use "Save project" and "Save as copy" to create separate feasibility models. Load any saved project from the Saved Projects screen.

Q: Does this work offline?
The app itself works offline. Charts require an internet connection (Chart.js loads from CDN). Exports require internet (PDF/Excel libraries load from CDN).
